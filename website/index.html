How it works

From the pulpit to your Monday morning in three steps.

  1. Capture the message

    Use whatever you have from Sunday.

    • RecordTap once when the sermon starts
    • NotesPhotograph your handwritten notes
    • TextPaste a transcript or an outline
    • YouTubePaste the link to your church's video

  2. Choose your pace

    Five days or seven. Five, fifteen or thirty minutes a day. Set it once and every study after that follows it.

  3. Open it each morning

    A reminder arrives at the time you pick. Read the day, mark it done, and by the next Sunday you have spent the whole week in the passage your pastor preached.

What you get

Every day has the same four parts.

Each one comes from the sermon you captured, so the week keeps returning to what your church heard together.

A passage
The Scripture for the day, with supporting verses from elsewhere in the Bible. Tap any reference to read it in full.
A devotional
A short reading that takes one idea from the message and stays with it.
A question
One thing to think over, or to write about, or to ask your spouse at dinner.
A prayer focus
A direction for prayer that follows from what you just read.

Also in the app

Photograph the bulletin. Get the calendar.

Take a picture of the paper bulletin and the app pulls out the events: what, when and where. Add the ones you want to your phone's calendar with a tap, so the men's breakfast doesn't stay folded in your Bible until it has passed.

A plain word about AI

The study is written by AI. Scripture is still the authority.

The app uses AI to turn the sermon into a week of study. AI can get things wrong, including a reference or a point of doctrine. Every passage links to the Bible so you can read it for yourself, and anything that sounds off is worth taking to your pastor.

It is a tool for staying in the Word your church is already preaching. It does not replace your Bible, your pastor or your church.

Questions

Before you sign up

Does it only work with one church or denomination?

No. The study is built from whatever message you give it, so it follows your church and your pastor.

Do I need permission to record the sermon?

Ask your church. Many are glad for you to record for personal study, and some would prefer you use their own recording. If your church posts its services on YouTube, you can paste that link and skip recording.

What if I missed Sunday?

Paste the YouTube link to the service, or a transcript, or a photo of someone's notes. You get the same study.

Can I read the passages in my own translation?

References open in the Bible online, where you can switch to the translation you read.
